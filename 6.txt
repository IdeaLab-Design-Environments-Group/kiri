STRAIN-AWARE ROUTING vs LENGTH-ONLY: WHAT THE CREASE PRICE BUYS

IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Switching the crease price off and re-routing the corpus says the strain-aware price is worth
keeping and cheaper than it looks: across the six patterns that carry fold angles, at 12 LEDs, it
removes 4.9% of the tension (mountain) crossings while total copper falls 2.1%. On the four plans
it actually changes, tension crossings fall 6.7% and copper falls 3.2%. It never causes a short
and never strands an LED. On `akde-hex` it takes three fewer mountain crossings and one more
valley -- the trade a model on |theta| cannot express, and the reason the sign is in the model.
Two patterns plan identically either way, and one, `akde-square-pyramid`, costs 16.9% more copper
for no fewer crossings; both are in the tables. The setting itself needs no defending: every route
is identical from 0.15 of the diagonal up to the full diagonal, so the shipped 0.5 sits on a
plateau rather than on a tuned peak.

Measured 2026-08-26 on the bundled corpus. Every number below was produced by a run made for
this report, not quoted from an earlier session, except where a line says otherwise.


0. WHAT WAS RUN, AND HOW TO RE-RUN IT

The comparison is the shipped router against itself with one term switched off. `planRoutes` now
takes an appended `creasePenaltyFrac` argument (default `FOLD_PENALTY_FRAC = 0.5`); at 0 a crease
costs nothing and the search minimises copper alone. That is the length-only baseline. It is a
parameter rather than a second copy of the router on purpose: a baseline routed by different code
from the thing it is a baseline for measures the fork as much as it measures the price.

Corpus: the eight bundled patterns in `public/examples/*.fkld`, battery on face 0, LEDs seated on
the first N distinct hinges, at N = 3, 6 and 12.

Two of the eight carry no fold angles at all (`bistable-star-tiling` has no traversable gap edge;
`kirigami-flap` records no dihedrals), so they fall back to the M/V/C classification and are
excluded from every strain figure below. Results are reported over the six patterns that carry
angles, and the two are listed so nobody counts them as agreement.

Crossings are counted by FACE TRANSITION -- sample the trace, ask which tile each sample is on,
count the changes -- not by segment intersection. This matters more than it sounds. A corridor
route passes exactly THROUGH a hinge midpoint, and a proper-crossing test does not register that
as a crossing. Counted the wrong way, `house` at three LEDs reads as 0 mountain crossings for the
length-only plan and 1 for the priced plan, which is the reverse of what the two plans do. Every
tile-to-tile transition that is not a priced crease was checked and is a flat facet diagonal
(`F`), interior to one printed tile: nothing folds there, so nothing is being missed.

Pinned as `tests/current/model/crease-price.test.ts`.


1. STRAIN-AWARE vs LENGTH-ONLY -- THE COMPARISON ITSELF

At 12 LEDs, over the six patterns that carry fold angles. "Tension" is mountain crossings, the
crossings the fatigue model exists to charge for; "compression" is valley crossings, which it
charges nothing.

  pattern                 copper (length-only -> priced)   tension   compression
  house                    11.54 ->   11.54    (+0.0%)      14 -> 14    0 ->  0
  church                   14.23 ->   15.07    (+5.9%)      16 -> 14    0 ->  0
  puffin                  763.23 ->  810.06    (+6.1%)      51 -> 49    0 ->  0
  akde-hex               1831.98 -> 1637.37   (-10.6%)      23 -> 20   12 -> 13
  akde-decagon-pyramid   1590.46 -> 1590.46    (+0.0%)      26 -> 26    8 ->  8
  akde-square-pyramid     313.56 ->  366.55   (+16.9%)      14 -> 14    6 ->  6
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  corpus total           4525.00 -> 4431.05    (-2.1%)     144 -> 137  26 -> 27

The headline: the crease price removes 4.9% of the tension crossings across the corpus and the
copper bill goes DOWN 2.1%. It is not a straight trade of copper for durability; on this corpus
it is closer to free.

Restricted to the plans the price actually changes (church, puffin, akde-hex, akde-square at 12
LEDs), tension crossings fall 104 -> 97, a 6.7% reduction, and copper falls 2923.0 -> 2829.1, a
3.2% reduction.

Two of the six plan identically with the price on and off. That is worth saying plainly rather
than burying: on `house` and `akde-decagon-pyramid` the strain term costs nothing and buys
nothing, because the routes those patterns want are the routes they get either way.

The one honest loss is `akde-square-pyramid`: +16.9% copper for exactly the same crossings, the
same creases, and the same strain-weighted exposure (16.562 either way). The price moved the plan
sideways there. It is one pattern in six and it is in the table rather than out of it.

The direction the model claims is visible on `akde-hex`, which is the case worth pointing at: the
priced plan takes THREE fewer mountain crossings and ONE more valley. That is the M/V asymmetry
doing exactly what it was put in for -- trading a crossing that fractures within a hundred cycles
for one that wrinkles and keeps conducting -- and a model on |theta| could not have made that
trade, because it cannot tell the two apart.

At 3 and 6 LEDs the corpus is barely distinguishable (tension 42 -> 41 and 71 -> 70, copper
within 0.2% both ways). The price bites when the sheet is crowded, which is when it matters.


2. CREASE CROSSING COUNT, SPLIT BY FOLD SENSE

Same runs, split out, so the length overhead in section 3 is legible against something.

  12 LEDs, six patterns    length-only    priced
  tension (mountain)          144          137     -4.9%
  compression (valley)         26           27     +3.8%
  cut (C)                       0            0
  total priced crossings      170          164     -3.5%

Cut crossings are zero under both conditions, on every pattern, at every LED count. That is the
containment rule doing the work, not the crease price: an opened cut is a hole in the silhouette
and `chordInside` refuses it, so the `C` branch of the price is never what keeps copper off one.

The strain-weighted exposure -- each crossing charged its own `creaseCostFraction` rather than
counted as one -- moves with the count almost exactly (e.g. puffin 51 -> 49 crossings, 51.0 ->
49.0 weighted). That is saturation, and it is the same finding `fold-strain.ts` records: past
about 12 degrees of fold on the default sheet the fatigue term is at its ceiling, so nearly every
real mountain on these patterns is charged full price. The only pattern where weighted and
counted differ is `akde-square-pyramid` (14 mountains + 6 valleys counting 16.562 weighted),
which is the one pattern with folds gentle enough to sit under the knee.


3. ROUTE LENGTH OVERHEAD

Completed by the length-only baseline, which is what was missing:

  - Corpus, 12 LEDs:                       -2.1%  (4525.00 -> 4431.05)
  - Plans that actually change:            -3.2%  (2923.00 -> 2829.05)
  - Worst single pattern:                 +16.9%  (akde-square-pyramid)
  - Best single pattern:                  -10.6%  (akde-hex)
  - Corpus at 3 LEDs / 6 LEDs:      +0.04% / +0.2%

"Overhead" turns out to be the wrong word for the aggregate. The crease price is not a detour
budget that buys durability; on a crowded pattern it is often the shorter plan as well, because
routing around a fold and routing around the copper already laid pull the same way.

There IS a real per-pattern cost -- 6% on church and puffin, 17% on akde-square-pyramid -- and
anyone reporting the aggregate alone is hiding those. Both are in the table.


4. WHERE THE PRICE STOPS MATTERING -- THE SETTING NEED NOT BE DEFENDED

Routes were hashed at crease prices of 0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0 of the pattern diagonal,
12 LEDs, six patterns. Every pattern gives byte-identical routes from 0.15 upward, including at
the full diagonal Nakaya et al. use. Only the band between 0 and 0.15 decides anything:

  pattern                 0        0.05     0.15 .. 1.0
  house                   A        A        A                 (never changes)
  church                  A        B        C
  puffin                  A        B        C
  akde-hex                A        A        B
  akde-decagon-pyramid    A        A        A                 (never changes)
  akde-square-pyramid     A        B        B

So `FOLD_PENALTY_FRAC = 0.5` is not a tuned constant that has to be justified to a reader. It is
one value on a plateau that runs from 0.15 to the full diagonal, and the earlier "at 0.15 the
penalty stops changing any route" is confirmed from the zero baseline upward. The same holds for
the fatigue constant itself: swept previously across an order of magnitude (eps 0.003 to 0.01)
with copper length and crossings identical on all six patterns.


5. ROUTING SUCCESS RATE

Bus router (two nets: PWR/GND), 12 LEDs, all eight patterns, both conditions: every LED that can
be seated is wired, and PWR x GND crossings -- shorts -- are ZERO on every pattern under both the
priced and the length-only plans. The crease price neither strands an LED nor causes a short.
Unwired LEDs are seating failures, not routing failures, and the split was checked rather than
assumed: on `akde-square-pyramid` all 8 of the 12 that go unwired are reported `unseated` -- the
1206 package will not sit on those hinges -- identically with the price on and off.
`kirigami-flap` and `bistable-star-tiling` have 1 and 0 traversable hinges, so they are asked for
1 and 0 LEDs and wire both.

Netlist router (N named nets, each joining its own terminals, none touching), five patterns at
two-to-five nets, three terminals per net -- 210 terminals:

  pattern                 2 nets   3 nets   4 nets   5 nets   total   orders tried
  house                    6/6      7/9      9/12    10/15    32/42   1/5/9/10
  church                   6/6      7/9      8/12     9/15    30/42   1/6/9/10
  akde-hex                 5/6      7/9      8/12     9/15    29/42   2/4/9/10
  puffin                   4/6      6/9      8/12     9/15    27/42   2/6/8/9
  akde-decagon-pyramid     4/6      6/9      7/12    11/15    28/42   2/5/7/10
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  total                   25/30    33/45    40/60    48/75   146/210 = 69.5%

At two nets the router reaches 25 of 30. The reach falls with net count -- 73% at three nets, 67%
at four, 64% at five -- and that is the open problem, stated as such: nets are kept apart by
exclusion, so each routes into the room the previous ones left.

What is NOT the open problem is ordering, and this is where the honest ceiling sits. The same
corpus reaches 146 of 210 with blame-directed reordering and 146 without it, while genuinely
exploring the alternatives (up to 10 distinct orderings per case, right-hand column). An
exhaustive sweep of all N! orderings reaches 191 of 252 against 189 for the four rotations shipped
-- two terminals in 252 is the entire remaining prize in ordering (that last pair of figures is
from the earlier sweep recorded in `net-routing.ts`, not re-run here). Some netlists are simply
not planar on one side of one sheet, and no ordering fixes one of those; a stranded terminal is
reported, with the net that is in its way named, rather than quietly shorted.


6. GEOMETRIC APPROXIMATION ERROR

Pure extraction: `dH` and `dHRel` are computed on every verification run and were read off, not
re-derived. `dH` is the Kabsch-aligned two-sided Hausdorff distance in millimetres between the
sheet folded FROM FLAT and the target, with declared vent holes excluded from coverage; `dHRel`
is that over the target's bounding-box diagonal, and the pipeline's acceptance gate is 5%.

  target                    dH (mm)   dHRel   eps (mm)   attempts   mean strain   crease resid.
  cube                       0.0000   0.000     8.6603       1         0.00000        0.00000
  open pyramid               0.0000   0.000     5.2202       1         0.00000        0.00000
  tent (free ridge)          0.0000   0.000     5.7879       1         0.00000        0.00000
  saddle fan (1 vent)        0.0000   0.000     6.9101       1         0.00000        0.00000
  sample-cube.stl            0.0000   0.000     8.6603       1         0.00000        0.00000
  sample-tetrahedron.stl     0.0000   0.000     8.6603       1         0.00000        0.00000
  sample-octahedron.stl      0.0000   0.000     8.6603       1         0.00000        0.00000
  sample-hex-pyramid.stl     0.0000   0.000     7.4833       1         0.00000        0.00000

Zero to four decimal places on all eight, every one on the first attempt, with zero mean bar
strain and zero crease residual: the sheet does not merely land near the target, it lands on it,
having travelled the whole fold path from flat rather than being placed at the answer. The
equilibrium pose -- released and settled, the secondary metric -- gives 0.0000 as well, so the
folded state is a stable one and not a pose held up by the transport.

What that number does and does not say, stated plainly: `dH` is measured against the TARGET MESH
the pipeline was handed. On these targets the tiles are planar and rigid, the fold angles are
exact, and the pattern is rigidly foldable onto the mesh -- so zero is the right answer and it is
a statement about the unfolding being exact, not about a smooth surface being well approximated.
Approximating a curved surface happens in the meshing upstream of this pipeline, and no figure
here bounds that.

INCOMPLETE, and reported as such: three further targets -- `saddle-roof`, `enneper` and
`lamp-dome` -- did not finish. `kirigamize(makeSaddleRoof(), {verify: true})` ran for roughly 14
minutes at full CPU without returning, and the repo's own acceptance test for it behaves the same
way -- `kirigamize-e2e.test.ts -t "saddle roof"` was left running for 11m41s at full CPU and
printed nothing before it was killed. Its 180-second timeout cannot fire, because the call is
synchronous and vitest cannot preempt a busy loop. So the three are absent from the table rather than filled in from an earlier
session, and the saddle-roof behaviour is worth a look on its own account: as things stand the
bundled acceptance suite cannot complete on this checkout.


7. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW

Stated so the table above is not read for more than it says.

- The material numbers are assumptions, not measurements taken on this system. `fatigueStrain =
  0.01` is an order-of-magnitude figure for rolled annealed copper foil in the low-cycle regime,
  and the substrate and foil moduli are handbook values. Everything above reads "given a sheet of
  these properties". What IS measured per pattern is the geometry: hinge width, fold angle, sign.
- No copper was folded. The evidence that tension crossings matter more than compression ones is
  Nakaya et al.'s fatigue test (SCF '25), not a fold-cycle test run here. This report measures
  what the router does with that model, not that the model is right.
- Two of the eight patterns record no fold angles and fall back to the M/V/C classification. They
  are excluded from every strain figure and named where they are excluded.
- The crease price is currently the only place strain reaches the plan. `strainLimit` -- refusing
  a crease outright -- is off by default, and for a measured reason: at a limit equal to the
  fatigue strain, 5 of 6 LEDs go unreachable on `house` and `church`.
- Nothing in `src/view` or `src/controller` constructs a `SheetSpec`; every production site takes
  `DEFAULT_SHEET`. The strain model is shipped in the sense that the shipped default routes
  through it, but a user cannot yet describe their own sheet from the app.
